The beam and objective are fixed; the stage carries the sample under them, so the image runs opposite to the control. Sweep at 5× to find a sample — at 50× the slide is twelve thousand fields wide — then switch up to measure, because a 5× objective collects only a few percent of the signal a 50× does. Frame and focus on the attenuated alignment beam; the excitation beam saturates the camera and you lose the picture. Focus into the bore, not onto the glass wall — a low-NA objective collects the capillary itself along with your sample. Write your coordinates down, because nothing here remembers them for you.

Leave the white light on and it will swamp the detector. The edge filter stops everything below 150 cm⁻¹ and all anti-Stokes light; to reach lattice modes or the anti-Stokes side, swap in the notch filters, which cover only a narrow window and let a wing of laser light through near the laser line. The laser is vertically polarized; the analyzer passes only the scattered light polarized parallel (V) or perpendicular (H) to it.
